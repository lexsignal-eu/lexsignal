# LexSignal — M9 Pilot Acceptance Programme

**Status: NOT READY FOR REAL CUSTOMER DATA OR COMMERCIAL CLAIMS.** Prepared 10 November 2026. No actual external interview, customer pilot, accuracy audit or subscription is claimed.

## Objective

Test whether a source-grounded, reviewer-controlled EU/Irish regulatory update workflow can save time for a *specific* organisation while preserving legal reliability and user control. First pilot category: small Irish regulatory/compliance advisory consultancy or Irish fintech risk function.

## Available demonstration

- Public source collection with freshness/cached status;
- 10 editorially curated law/initiative references, 17 milestones;
- two synthetic company-profile lenses with explainable candidate shortlists;
- source-linked example research worksheet, researcher-selected Markdown export;
- existing local review workspace and action register;
- deterministic CI/static release manifest, manual legal validation still required.

**Known limitations:** no provision-level verified text, legal amendment engine, tenant accounts, multi-user review, customer upload security, browser acceptance study, privacy-reviewed SLA, or payment capability. No product demonstration should imply these features exist.

## Discovery / usability protocol (3–5 consenting testers)

Use voluntary sessions, with public/synthetic demonstration data only. Do not access or upload their internal records. For each session record consent, professional role (without identity disclosure), scope, completion, observed blockers, source-citation quality, task completion and product feedback.

### Test route
1. Pick synthetic Irish payment institution; identify its assumptions and open questions.
2. Explain why DORA/GDPR/other candidates were initially shortlisted; reject or defer at least one when scope is uncertain.
3. Follow source-specific official URLs and distinguish original OJ text from current/consolidated law.
4. Inspect source age, feed failures and unreviewed classifications.
5. Select checked items and export a Markdown research worksheet.
6. In the existing workspace, manually assess a current official publication, assign a next step and create a draft brief.
7. Check mobile layout and keyboard-only navigation. Repeat with failed/empty data where feasible.

### Scoring
- Scenario completion: at least 4 of 5 critical actions completed by each tester without assistance.
- Source citation accuracy: zero fabricated source links, fake provision quotations or false repeal assertions.
- Applicability claims: zero automatic unreviewed statements that a specific organisation is bound by a duty.
- Understandability: each tester correctly identifies a research candidate as **unverified**, not an approved legal conclusion.
- Accessibility: no critical keyboard/mobility blocker.
- Data handling: no personal/customer file is submitted during these sessions.
- Reliability: tested failure and cached-source states are clearly visible.
- Report quality: users can open each cited source and identify remaining verification questions.

These are targets, **not** measured results.

## What to capture

Create a GitHub issue per reproducible blocker: expected/actual, URL or feature, browser/device, timestamp, steps to reproduce and severity. Do not publish personal names or confidential content. Record session outcomes in private, access-controlled systems before sharing a redacted summary.

## Go / no-go conditions

**No-go** while any of the following remain:
- Unverifiable legal citations, incorrect effective dates or unsupported applicability assertions;
- browser journey fails on a key use case;
- privacy/security assessment incomplete for intended customer data;
- no verified seller identity, fiscal/social welfare treatment, contract/terms and billing setup for a paid pilot;
- missing incident and support escalation owner.

A *public demo with synthetic examples* may be used for consented feedback despite these gaps. Real documents and billing may not.
