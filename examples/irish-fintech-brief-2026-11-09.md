# LexSignal | Irish digital-finance regulatory intelligence
**Demonstration brief • 9 November 2026 • Target reader: Irish payment/e-money institution compliance teams**

## Executive assessment

The Central Bank of Ireland's October 2026 action involving Coinbase Ireland is a practical case study for safeguarding and intra-group outsourcing oversight. The EDPB's September 2026 publications provide separate prompts for GDPR governance and, where applicable, DSA-facing services. The amended AI Act transitional timetable should be monitored by providers of potentially covered synthetic-content generating AI systems. This note is a targeted demonstration, not a complete update of all applicable requirements.

### 1. Safeguarding / outsourcing: enforcement signal

**Primary source:** [Central Bank of Ireland — Coinbase Ireland, 22 October 2026](https://edit.centralbank.ie/news/article/press-release-central-bank-takes-enforcement-action-against-coinbase-ireland-22-october-2026)

The Central Bank announced a **€3,252,618** penalty following a settlement relating to safeguarding and outsourcing failures under the Electronic Money Regulations 2011. Its findings included inadequate Irish-entity oversight of outsourced group arrangements, deficiencies in system design and control testing, and insufficient escalation of material safeguarding changes. The sanctions were stated to be subject to High Court confirmation.

**Potentially relevant firms:** authorised e-money institutions and payment firms with safeguarding functions and outsourced or intra-group arrangements.

**Suggested internal follow-up:** evidence of accountable local governance; review of reconciliation controls and testing; documented escalation thresholds; assessment of third-party/intra-group accountability. These are review prompts, not a conclusion that any other firm has breached a duty.

### 2. GDPR: distinguish a consultation from final EDPB guidance

**Primary source:** [EDPB announcement, 21 September 2026](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/edpb-harmonises-fining-methodology-and-adopts-final-dsa-gdpr-guidelines_en)

The EDPB announced guidelines concerning administrative fines relative to other corrective powers, **subject to public consultation until 13 November 2026**. In the same announcement, it adopted **final guidelines** concerning interactions between the Digital Services Act and GDPR.

**Suggested internal follow-up:** catalogue the two documents separately; do not classify the consultation-stage guidance as binding adopted law; consider final DSA–GDPR guidance for any in-scope intermediary services.

### 3. AI-generated content: targeted transition date

**Primary source:** [European Commission — AI Act implementation timeline](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/eu-ai-act-implementation-timeline)

**2 December 2026** is an amended transitional deadline for certain synthetic-content generating AI-system providers whose systems were already placed on the market before 2 August 2026 to meet the relevant Article 50(2) requirements. The milestone does not apply universally to all businesses using AI.

**Suggested internal follow-up:** inventory in-scope generation features, confirm provider/deployer role, relevant placing-on-market dates, and applicability of the Article 50(2) obligations.

## Editorial limitations

Check the latest legislative text, procedural status and business-specific applicability before external distribution.
